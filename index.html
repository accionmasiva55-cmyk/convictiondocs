Overview

Product Core Mechanics

Core Mechanics

1. BACK / FADE

Every tweet from a KOL becomes a two-sided market. There is no “maybe”: you BACK the call if you think it lands, or FADE it if you think it does not. Both sides stay open for as long as the market is live.

Prices are quoted in cents, and every share settles at $1.00 if its side is right and $0.00 if it is wrong. That makes the price the market’s implied probability: BACK trading at 70¢ means the crowd is pricing a 70% chance the call lands. The two sides always add up to $1.00 — if BACK is 70¢, FADE is 30¢.

Your downside is capped at what you paid. Buy 100 BACK shares at 70¢ and you stake $70 to win $100. Take the other side and $30 wins the same $100. The cheaper the side, the bigger the payoff for being right — markets reward clarity, not noise.

2. Market lifecycle

Every market goes through the same five stages: the tweet is detected (by a curator or via PROVE IT.), the market opens with an explicit resolution criteria, the trading window opens, the market resolves by verifying the real-world outcome, and winners get paid while every participant's Conviction Score updates.

Resolution Criteria

Defined rigidly at market inception by Curators or Level 10+ users. It uses verifiable data sources (e.g., Coingecko for prices, official announcements for events). No room for interpretation.

Deleted Tweets

We keep the receipts. The market settles based on the exact original wording captured at creation. If a KOL deletes or edits their tweet, the market remains active and resolves normally.

Time Window

Typically ranges from 24 hours (for fast-paced CT drama) to 6 months (for macro calls). The exact lock and resolution times are always visible before you place a trade.

Marketing & Community GTM Strategy

GTM Strategy

We are not launching a new behavior. CT already makes calls, piles on and argues about who was right — we are launching the scoreboard it never had.

That is the whole go-to-market advantage and it should shape every decision. There is no market to educate and no habit to build. The pitch is one sentence long, it lands in the same breath as the tweet it is about, and the person hearing it was already having the argument.

So the job is not awareness. The job is to be present at the exact moment a take gets posted, and to make taking a side cost less effort than replying to it.

The wedge

We do not go after “crypto traders”. That audience is over-served and expensive. We go after the people who are already spending their day being loud about calls, in this order.

Marketing & Community Twitter Strategy

Twitter Strategy

The account is not a brand account. It is a participant in the argument that happens to run the book.

Brand accounts post announcements and get ignored. We post markets: a call, a price, and two sides you can take right now. Every post is a thing to do, not a thing to read.

The tone follows from that. We are never the neutral exchange explaining its product — we are the one keeping score, and we are slightly enjoying it. Confident, short, funny when the timeline is being ridiculous, and never preachy about risk.

Quote the call. State the odds. Give the action. Nothing else earns its place in the tweet.

The KOL pitch

We are not asking them to shill anything, and the pitch should never sound like we are. A market on their call already exists — the conversation is about what that gets them.

The offer is a permanent, public, verifiable track record. Every KOL claims they called it. Only the ones on CONVICTION can point at the receipt.

Objections and answers
  • “So you are betting against me.”
    The market prices the call, it does not take a side. And a market at 70% BACK is the timeline publicly agreeing with you.
  • “What if I am wrong in public?”
    You already are — screenshots exist. The difference is that here the wins are recorded with the same permanence as the misses.
  • “What is in it for me?”
    Being tradeable is status. A market with your name on it is distribution you did not have to post for, and copy-trading turns a high score into followers who act.
  • “Take my tweet down.”
    Handled human to human, not by policy. But the wording is captured at creation and the market settles either way — that is what makes the record worth anything.

Recruiting curators

The job is taste, not effort. A curator has to spot the difference between a spicy take and a tradeable one — the second has a number and a date in it.

What we look for

  • Lives on CT and reads it before breakfast
  • Instinct for which take will still be argued about tomorrow
  • Writes resolution criteria that nobody can wriggle out of
  • Knows the second tier of KOLs, not just the top ten

The onboarding

  • Brief on the five categories and the resolution bar
  • Shadow week: submissions reviewed before going live
  • Own the daily market of the day for one category
  • Level 45 grants Curator status inside the product itself

Marketing & Community Referral Program

Referral Program

The mechanic is simple and already decided: $25 each. This page is about the part that actually determines whether it works — when we ask.

Referral programs fail on timing, not on reward size. Asking at signup is asking someone to vouch for a product they have not used. Asking in a banner is asking nobody in particular.

There is exactly one moment when a user wants to bring someone in, and it is the same moment they want to post: right after they were right in public.

Where the ask lives

Moment Why here

On a KOL battle market These are the markets people want an audience for. “Bring someone who disagrees.”
Empty portfolio state Nothing to show yet, so give them something to do that still earns.

The creative

The invite is a dare, not a discount code. It should read like something one degen sends another, because that is exactly what it is.

Sounds like us
  • “I just took $30 off this take. Your turn.”
  • “You have been wrong about this for a month. Prove it.”
  • “$25 each. Come fade me.”
Does not

  • “Sign up with my referral link”

Keep it honest

The reward only fires on a real deposit and a real trade, which is what keeps the program from turning into farmed signups. Watch the ratio of referrals to second trades — if invited users never come back, the program is buying noise, not users.

Marketing & Community Launch Campaign

Launch Campaign

One idea, carried the whole way through: every tweet is a market. We do not announce that. We demonstrate it on the loudest calls in the timeline until people work it out.

The beats

Not signups and not impressions. Receipts posted to X per day. It is the only metric that is simultaneously an engagement measure, a retention measure and the acquisition channel.

Tokenomics & Business Revenue Streams

Revenue Streams

Right now CONVICTION makes nothing, on purpose. Revenue is a switch we flip once the board is busy, not a thing we optimize on day one.

The business is a take on volume. That is the whole model, and everything else on this page is a second-order surface built on top of it. A prediction market with liquidity has an obvious way to make money; a prediction market without it has no business at all, which is why the launch window runs at zero.

The important consequence: the growth mechanics and the revenue mechanics are the same mechanics. Receipts, levels and the Conviction Score all push toward more trades settled, and every trade settled is the unit we eventually take a cut of.

Where the money comes from

One primary stream and a set of surfaces that only become available once volume exists. The status column is honest about what is decided and what is not.

Stream How it works Status
Trading fee A take on settled volume. The core model. Level discounts up to 10% already exist to soften it for the people who built the place. Core model
Copy-trade cut Copying a KOL is a premium action, not a basic one. A cut on copied flow, optionally shared with the KOL being copied. V1.5
Sponsored markets A protocol, exchange or launch pays for a market on its own thesis. Native advertising that is indistinguishable from the product. Proposal
Reputation data The Conviction Score is a verified track record for a whole cohort of CT. Read access for other apps, leaderboards and terminals. Proposal

Still to be decided

The fee exists in the product already — the Level System hands out discounts against it. What it is a discount on has not been set. Three decisions, and they compound.

  • The base rate
    Every level discount in the product is a percentage of a number nobody has set yet.
  • What it is charged on
    On the stake at entry, or on the winnings at settlement. Charging winners only reads far better on a product whose whole story is being right.
  • When the window closes
    Zero fees is a promise with no stated end date. It needs one before it becomes an expectation.

The thing not to do

Do not tax the loop. Posting a receipt, creating a market with PROVE IT. and referring someone are the three actions that acquire users for free. Any fee that touches them is a fee on our own distribution.

Tokenomics & Business Unit Economics

Unit Economics

The launch stack is expensive by design. Knowing exactly how expensive is what tells us when it has to end.

Every incentive on the platform is a deliberate purchase of one thing: a user who has traded. We pay for the deposit, we pay for the fear of the first loss, we pay for the repetition and we pay for the invite. Added together they define the ceiling on what a single activated user can cost us.

That ceiling is only survivable because it is a launch window, and because the second cohort is supposed to arrive through receipts rather than through the treasury.

Tokenomics & Business Points Program

Points Program

Design · V2.0

We are not going to launch a points program. We already have one — it just does not pay out yet.

Most projects bolt points on as a retroactive bribe and users can tell. CONVICTION has been keeping score since the first trade: XP measures how much you played, the Level records how far that got you, and the Conviction Score measures whether you were any good. Three numbers, already earned, already public.

So the points program is not a new mechanic. It is the decision about which of these existing numbers converts into ownership, and at what weight.

The substrate already exists

Layer Measures Farmable?
XP Raw activity, capped daily on almost every action. Partly — that is what the caps are for.
Level 1–50 Accumulated commitment. Only ever goes up. Slowly, and it costs real volume to do it.
Conviction Score Accuracy. Win rate, volume and consistency, and it falls when you are wrong. No. You cannot farm being right.

Design principles

Four rules that follow directly from what the product already rewards. Break any of them and the points program starts contradicting the thing it is supposed to reward.

  • 01
    Accuracy gates volume, not the other way round
    The Conviction Score should multiply points earned, so wash-trading your way to the top pays worse than being right at a tenth of the size. Markets reward clarity, not noise.
  • 02
    Reward the loop, not the login
    Receipts posted, markets created and referrals that actually traded are the actions that grow the platform. Those should out-earn passive activity by a wide margin.
  • 03
    Retroactive, from day one
    Every level and every score earned during the MVP counts. The people who traded on an empty board took the most risk and should be weighted accordingly.
  • 04
    Never announce the formula in advance
    A published formula is a farming spec. Publish the principles, publish the behaviors that count, keep the weights until distribution.

Open decisions

  • Do points sit on top of XP as a separate ledger, or is XP itself the points balance?
  • Are they seasonal, resetting with a leaderboard each period, or cumulative for the life of the account?
  • Does a falling Conviction Score claw points back, or only slow the rate at which you earn them?
  • What share of supply the program represents — which is a token question, not a points one.
Tokenomics & Business Token Utility

Token Utility

Design · V2.0

One rule decides everything on this page: no utility the product does not already have.

CONVICTION does not need a token to work. Trading, levels, receipts and the Conviction Score all function without one, and that is a strength — it means a token can be introduced from a position of having something to distribute rather than something to fund.

It also sets the bar. Every candidate utility below is something the platform already does and already gates. The token changes who controls it, not whether it exists. Anything that fails that test is a mechanic invented to give the token a job, and users can smell it immediately.

Candidate utilities

Utility What it maps to today Verdict
Fee discounts Already exists as a Level perk, 1% at Level 15 up to 10% at Legend. Strong
Market creation rights PROVE IT. is gated at Level 10 with a 5-a-day cap. A real, already-scarce resource. Strong
Resolution disputes Today a settlement is decided by the stated source. A staked challenge process is the natural decentralised version. Evaluate
Curator selection Curators are hired, and Level 45 grants the status in-product. Could become an elected, staked role. Evaluate
Copy-trade revenue share V1.5 feature with no payout model yet. A way for a high Conviction Score to earn directly. Evaluate
Trading currency Nothing. Markets settle in dollars and onboarding runs through Apple Pay. Reject

Non-negotiables

  • The token never sits between a user and their first trade. Frictionless onboarding is an MVP promise and a token requirement would break it.
  • Markets do not settle in it. Payouts stay in dollars — a receipt that says “+$30.00” means something, one denominated in a volatile asset does not.
  • The Conviction Score is never purchasable. The moment reputation can be bought, the entire product is worthless.
  • It ships after the loop is proven, not to bootstrap it. V2.0 exists precisely so the token is a distribution event, not a funding one.

Not covered here

Supply, allocation, vesting, chain and launch mechanism are all undecided and deliberately absent. This page is the utility argument only — the numbers come after the loop has been proven, not before.

Design UI / UX Principles

UI / UX Principles

The product competes with a reply box. If taking a side is harder than typing “ngmi”, we lose.

That is the design constraint everything else falls out of. Not “make it beautiful” and not “make it powerful” — make the distance between seeing a take and having a position on it shorter than the distance between seeing it and arguing about it.

01

Two choices, never three

There is no “maybe” anywhere in the interface. BACK and FADE are always the same size, the same weight and the same distance from the thumb. Neutrality in the layout, not in the color.

02

The tweet is the interface

Markets are rendered as the tweet they came from, not as a row in an exchange. The claim, the handle and the avatar do the explaining — a market that needs a subtitle to make sense was written wrong.

03

Price is the only chart

One number and one bar: BACK at 70¢, FADE at 30¢. No order book, no depth chart, no candles. This is not a terminal for traders, it is a terminal for the timeline.

04

Onboarding ends at the first trade

No tour, no tutorial, no wallet setup screen. Apple Pay to fund, one tap to take a side, and every launch incentive exists to make that first tap free of consequence. A user who has not traded has not onboarded.

05

Reputation is always on screen

Level badge and Conviction Score sit next to every handle, everywhere — feed, market, leaderboard, receipt. Your reputation is your portfolio, so it should never be a page you have to go and find.

06

The loss is designed as carefully as the win

BAD CALL gets the same production value as HE CALLED IT. It is shareable, it is not apologetic, and it never gets hidden behind a dismissible toast. A scoreboard that only celebrates is an ad.

Anti-patterns

  • A confirmation modal on a $10 trade. The stake is the confirmation.
  • Financial-app chrome: portfolios in the nav, P&L greetings, candlestick anything.
  • Green and red used for anything other than BACK and FADE. Not for success toasts, not for validation errors.
  • An empty feed. If there is nothing to trade, the design has already failed — curators exist to prevent this state.

Design User Flows

User Flows

Four flows carry the entire product. Each one is listed with the step where we actually lose people, because that is the step worth designing.

1 · First session

From a cold link to a settled trade. Almost always entered sideways — someone tapped a receipt on X, not a homepage.

Taps a receipt on X Lands on the market Picks a side Apple Pay deposit Order fills Receipt

Drop-off: the deposit. They chose a side before paying, which is the right order — the intent is already spent. Every launch incentive is aimed at this exact step, and it should be surfaced here rather than at signup.

Note the order: side first, wallet second. Asking for money before a user has an opinion is asking a stranger for a favor.

2 · The returning trade

The loop that has to be effortless, because it is the one that happens hundreds of times.

Push: market resolved Opens feed BACK / FADE Size on the sheet Filled

Drop-off: sizing. Any hesitation here is a trade lost. Preset amounts, last-used default, no confirmation dialog. Target is two taps from feed to filled.

3 · The receipt

The growth loop, rendered as a flow. This is the most commercially important sequence in the product and it happens after the money has already moved.

Market settles Receipt minted One tap to X Score updates Invite prompt

Drop-off: the share. If posting takes more than one tap, the acquisition channel closes. The image, the copy and the link are pre-composed — the user edits, they never author.

4 · PROVE IT.

Unlocks at Level 10. The only flow in the product with a review step in the middle, and the only one where friction is a feature.

Paste tweet URL Write the question Pick source & deadline Review Live · +25 XP

Drop-off: writing the question. Turning a take into a falsifiable claim is genuinely hard. Templates per category do most of the work — the user should be filling in an asset, a number and a date, not composing from a blank field.

Tech & Architecture Architecture

Architecture

Six layers. A tweet goes in one end, a receipt comes out the other, and a ledger keeps score the whole way through.

Two things make this different from a generic prediction market. The first is that our market supply comes from an external platform we do not control, which makes ingestion a first-class concern rather than an admin form. The second is that reputation is a product surface, not analytics — the Conviction Score has to be correct, live and public, which means it needs a real event ledger behind it and not a nightly job.

01 · Ingestion
Tweet capture
Pulls the tweet, snapshots the exact wording, author and timestamp. Immutable from here on.
02 · Market service
Claim, source, deadline
Turns a snapshot into a market with resolution criteria attached. Curated or via PROVE IT.
03 · Trading & ledger
Positions and balances
Order fills, share accounting, bonus versus real funds. The one component that must never lose a write.
04 · Settlement
Resolve and pay
Reads the declared source at the deadline, settles every share at $1.00 or $0.00, credits balances.
05 · Reputation
Score, XP, levels
Consumes settlement events. Recomputes the Conviction Score, applies XP with caps and multipliers.
06 · Distribution
Receipts out to X
Renders the receipt image and the share intent. This is the acquisition channel, so it is infrastructure.

Hard constraints

  • 01
    The ledger is the source of truth
    Every balance change is an append-only event: deposit, bonus credit, fill, settlement, protection refund, referral payout. Balances are derived, never edited.
  • 02
    Bonus funds are a separate balance
    The $100 match is non-withdrawable, so it cannot be one number added to the wallet. It needs its own bucket with its own spend and withdrawal rules from day one — retrofitting this is painful.
  • 03
    Resolution is a stored decision, not a live lookup
    Settlement records the value read, the source and the timestamp. A dispute must be answerable months later without re-querying anything.
  • 04
    The tweet snapshot outlives the tweet
    We keep the receipts, which means storing the text and author at creation. If the market renders by re-fetching, a deleted tweet takes the market with it.
  • 05
    Receipt rendering is server-side
    The share image has to exist as a URL before X can unfurl it. A client-side canvas screenshot is not a growth channel.

  • Buy the payment and identity layers, build the market, ledger and reputation layers. Those three are the product.
  • Optimize for shipping V1.5 and V2.0 without a rewrite: user-generated markets, copy-trading, bots and native apps are all known and all hit the same core services.
  • Assume the X API will get more expensive or more restricted. Isolate it behind one service so a change of ingestion strategy is not a change of product.

Team Structure

The org chart is the loop. Every role owns one step of it, and the handoffs between them are where things go wrong.

CONVICTION runs a cycle: find the take, list it, get it traded, settle it honestly, turn the outcome into a receipt, and let the receipt bring the next person in. A team this small cannot be organized by function — it has to be organized by which part of that cycle stalls without someone watching it.

    The launch stack tops out at $275 a user. If receipt-driven signups do not arrive, that is a treasury drain with a fixed end date and no revenue behind it.

    A large account deciding publicly that being traded is harassment. Handled by relationships and tone, not by policy — see the objections section of KOL onboarding.

The one that matters most

Settlement integrity. Everything the product sells — the score, the receipts, the whole reputation layer — is worth exactly as much as people’s belief that markets resolve honestly. It is the only risk on this page that cannot be recovered from.

Team & Operations Legal

Legal

Open · counsel required

Read this first

This page is not legal advice and records no decisions. It is the list of questions that have to be answered by qualified counsel in each jurisdiction before launch, written down so that nobody assumes they are already settled. Every item below is open.

A real-money market on a public prediction is a regulated activity in most places, and which regulator applies depends on facts we have not fixed yet.

Questions for counsel

  • What are we, legally?
    Prediction markets get classified as derivatives, as gaming, or as neither, and the answer differs by country. Everything else on this page depends on it.
  • Which jurisdictions do we serve, and which do we block?
    Geo-restriction has to be designed into onboarding, not bolted on. It also constrains the payment processor.
  • How much KYC, and at what threshold?
    Directly in tension with the frictionless onboarding promise. Where the check sits in the flow is a product decision with a legal floor.
  • Do the bonuses change our classification?
    Deposit matches, loss protection and referral payments are all regulated promotional constructs in gambling-adjacent regimes, with their own disclosure rules.
  • Can we list a market on a named person’s tweet?
    Uses their handle, their words and their likeness commercially without consent. Quotation, publicity rights and defamation exposure all need a view — particularly for the Meme & Culture category, where markets are about people rather than prices.
  • What is the takedown process?
    A subject demanding removal of a live market with real money in it. The product answer is that markets resolve regardless — that needs to survive a lawyer’s letter, and it needs a documented process.
  • Who is liable for a user-generated market?
    PROVE IT. in V1.5, and open creation in V2.0, move authorship to users. Intermediary protections vary and the review step is part of the answer.
  • What does the token trigger?
    A V2.0 question, but the points program starts accruing long before it. Retroactive distribution to users who earned points expecting one is the part that needs early advice.

What the product owes regardless

Independent of how the classification lands, these are table stakes for a real-money product and should be scoped now rather than after.

  • Clear terms covering how markets resolve, what happens in a dispute, and that a settled outcome is final.
  • Bonus terms stated plainly: the $100 match is non-withdrawable, and first loss protection is once per account up to $100.
  • An age gate, and self-exclusion or limit tooling if the gaming classification applies anywhere we operate.
  • Data handling for the tweets, handles and profile data we store — including what happens to a market when a source account is deleted.
  • A stated end date for the zero-fee window, so switching fees on is a schedule rather than a surprise.

The argument

The behavior already exists. There is no market to educate and no habit to build. KOLs make calls, the timeline piles on and everyone claims they called it — every day, for free. We are not asking anyone to do something new, only to put money behind what they were already saying.

The wedge is cultural, not financial. Prediction markets that chase news and institutions are competing on liquidity and regulation. We compete on being native to a specific timeline, which is a position that cannot be bought with a bigger balance sheet.

Acquisition is a product feature. Every settled trade produces a receipt, and every receipt is a post with proof attached. The growth channel is manufactured by usage rather than purchased — which is why the metric we run the company on is receipts posted per day.

Retention is structural. Balances are portable, reputation is not. A Conviction Score and a Legend badge exist nowhere else, so the longer someone plays the more expensive leaving becomes.
